1. Decision and sensitivity

Decision. Should a California industrial facility approve a $48,000 controls upgrade when the vendor forecasts 120,000 kWh of annual electricity savings?

Public benchmark. EIA reports 24.12¢/kWh as California’s average industrial retail electricity price for August 2026. Use it to illustrate sensitivity. To estimate site savings, substitute the facility’s avoidable usage charges and assess demand charges separately.

Source: EIA Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.A, California industrial sector

Gross annual benefit = forecast kWh × rate. Simple payback = installed cost ÷ gross annual benefit. This example excludes operating costs, maintenance, financing, rebates, and demand savings.

Recommended decision

Request a documented baseline and a site-tariff calculation before signing. Ask the vendor to specify how the savings estimate changes with production and schedule changes.

2. Evidence to request and how to check results

Before approval
Obtain the itemized installation quote, 12 months of bills, meter boundary, operating schedules, production totals, and the vendor’s calculation workbook.
At handoff
Record the approved savings forecast and agree on the measurement window, data owner, comparison method, and acceptance threshold.
After implementation
Compare normalized usage over the agreed period and document relevant changes in volume, weather, and runtime. Investigate discrepancies before attributing savings to the upgrade.

Illustrative outcome. If comparable annual savings are measured at 84,000 kWh against the 120,000 kWh forecast, realization is 70%. The measured site value depends on the actual avoidable rate and the quality of the comparison.
