xAI Colossus, Memphis: a public-evidence infrastructure desk analysis.

This case demonstrates how Trusta ORBIT separates what public evidence supports from what remains unknown across grid power, internal electrical infrastructure, water, wastewater reuse, permitting, and expansion timing. It is not an xAI engagement, endorsement, engineering design, or customer result.

Executive finding

The most valuable conclusion is not a fabricated “first constraint.”

Public records show material external dependencies for both power delivery and water-reuse infrastructure, but they do not expose enough private facility data to name one definitive first-binding constraint without inventing inputs.

Power is a real schedule dependency

MLGW documented 150 MW of service and a second 150 MW request tied to transmission upgrades, another substation, and TVA approval. ORBIT treats these as dated milestone dependencies—not as proof of IT capacity or site readiness.

Water strategy evolved materially

Official public materials moved from an early potable-water baseline to a shared recycled-water strategy, with planning figures evolving from about 10.4 MGD to 13 MGD. ORBIT preserves source dates rather than silently choosing one number.

Hydraulic capacity is not regulatory readiness

City material separately identified a Reclaimed Wastewater Management Plan / operating-permit dependency. ORBIT keeps infrastructure capacity and authorization as distinct evidence gates.

Evidence discipline

What the official public record supports.

Electrical service and expansion

MLGW reported 8 MW from a pre-existing substation plus 142 MW from a newly constructed substation, for 150 MW total, and described a second 150 MW request requiring identified transmission upgrades, another substation, and TVA approval.

Recycled-water infrastructure

City of Memphis material described a planned 10.4 MGD reuse plant, a future expected xAI demand of 5 MGD, and combined peak demand of 12.5 MGD for xAI, TVA and Nucor. Later MLGW material described a 13 MGD recycled-water facility, showing that the public design basis evolved.

Permitting remains a separate gate

The City document stated that TDEC requested a Reclaimed Wastewater Management Plan for the operating permit. TDEC's current guidance also makes clear that data centers may have project-specific water, wastewater, stormwater, air and other environmental requirements.

What ORBIT refuses to infer

Public service MW does not establish usable IT MW, internal electrical adequacy, cooling sufficiency, or ROI. Reuse-plant MGD does not establish exclusive xAI allocation, current commissioning status, or regulatory approval. Those remain evidence requests.

Cross-resource map

One project, multiple independent decision gates.

Grid power

Utility service, transmission upgrades, substation delivery, approval milestones, curtailment conditions.

Internal electrical

Transformer and switchgear ratings, N-1 / usable operating envelope, peak loading. Not established by public utility MW alone.

Cooling

Heat rejection, cooling architecture, redundancy, power-water tradeoffs, thermal headroom. Material private inputs are not public.

Water

Potable baseline, recycled-water production, allocation among users, current demand profile, operating envelope.

Wastewater / reuse

Feedwater availability, treatment quality, conveyance, reuse specifications, discharge and pretreatment constraints.

Permitting

Separate state/local environmental and operating requirements. Physical capacity does not equal authorization.

Next evidence

What would convert this desk analysis into a quantitative site screen.

1 · Electrical

Current utility-service confirmation, milestone dates, internal one-line, equipment ratings, N-1 basis and measured peak loading.

2 · Cooling & water

Cooling architecture, heat-rejection basis, design and measured operating points, current reuse capacity/allocation, demand profile and water-quality specifications.

3 · Schedule & economics

Phase ramp, project schedule, cost estimates, operating baseline and decision alternatives. Only then should ORBIT quantify timing, CAPEX/OPEX and YIELD-stage value.
